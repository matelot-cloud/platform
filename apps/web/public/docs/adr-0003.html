ADR 0003 — Two-level access control (GitLab model)

Status: accepted · Date: 2026-09-18

Decision

Access control has two levels, like GitLab's instance admins + group/project access levels.

  1. Cell level (instance). Every user is user or admin (users.platform_role). Administrators see and can do everything in the cell and own the Administration area (users, and from step 02/03 on: integrations, runners, cell settings). Users see nothing until they hold a role somewhere. Admins can disable a user (they keep their memberships but cannot sign in). Bootstrap emails (BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_EMAILS) become administrators on first sign-in and owners (ORG_ADMIN) of the root organization.
  2. Scoped level. Memberships = user × role × scope at organization, workspace, team, project, service or environment. A role applies to its scope and everything below (groups → subgroups → projects); the effective permissions at a point are the union of all roles up the chain (GitLab's "highest access level wins"); nothing flows upward; a member can always see the path to what they own. Roles are the concept's eleven (ORG_ADMIN = owner of a subtree, TEAM_LEAD ≈ maintainer, DEVELOPER, READ_ONLY ≈ reporter, plus the security/compliance roles).

Why

  • Enterprise expectation and a model every developer already understands.
  • The cell admin is the operator's and customer IT's handle on the instance without granting organization roles that show up in security dashboards.
  • Scoped roles keep least privilege for everyone else, including the AI (step 15 inherits them).

Consequences

  • AccessContext.isPlatformAdmin short-circuits AuthorizationService; every listing that filters by visibility automatically includes everything for admins.
  • Guards must not assume an admin holds any membership; audit shows admin actions as the user.
  • Cell-level actions (user.*) are audited without an organization id.
